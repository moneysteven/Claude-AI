Sunsets

The sunsetspot.

As Rick’s tells it, it became the first public bar and restaurant of its type on Negril’s West End cliffs: an alternative to Seven Mile Beach, and a place to gather for the sunset.

Clear skies aren’t guaranteed, and some evenings the clouds win. On a good night, though, the whole terrace turns to watch.

Music & atmosphere

Turn itup, mon.

Music has long been part of an evening at Rick’s. The Jamaica Observer described a live reggae band in 2015, and recent visitors talk about live music or a DJ, dancing and a lively crowd.

Rick’s doesn’t publish a nightly line-up, so the sound on any given night can vary. Ask the team when you arrive. Expect it to be busy and lively, especially around sunset.

The cliffs

The famouscliffs.

Watching the jumpers is part of the Rick’s legend. If you’re thinking about it yourself, read this first.

The official FAQ asks “Can I jump off the cliffs?” and answers: “Yes, At your own risk.” It lists these jump levels and water depth:

10 ft3 m level
25 ft7.6 m level
35 ft10.6 m level
15 ft4.5 m water depth

Source: official Rick’s FAQ (undated, read 7 Nov 2026). A third-party guide, ArrivalGuides, describes a 30-foot cliff.

{{ico:alert}}Before anyone jumps

  • These are published figures, not a promise about today. Tides, swell, weather and clearance change.
  • Check current conditions and follow staff guidance on site. If staff say not now, it’s not now.
  • Jumping is entirely at your own risk. This website doesn’t encourage anyone to jump, least of all children or anyone who isn’t a strong, confident swimmer.
  • Prefer to watch? The terraces are the best seats in the house.
